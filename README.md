Fire Protection System Replacement: Mapping Fire Watch Coverage Across Cutover States

Replacing a fire alarm or sprinkler system looks a lot like a production migration. There's a legacy system, a new system, a cutover window, a period where both are partially working, and an acceptance test before the old one is fully retired. The difference is that the "downtime" in this migration leaves people and property without fire protection. This breakdown maps fire watch coverage to each state of a replacement project and covers what to check when choosing among the best fire watch companies near me.

## Why Replacement Projects Need Explicit Coverage Planning

During a replacement, protection status changes zone by zone and day by day. A floor might be fully protected on Monday, impaired Tuesday through Thursday, and partially restored Friday. Without explicit planning, gaps appear: a zone goes offline before the fire watch starts, or the watch is released before the new zone is accepted.

The rules don't pause for the project. Under commonly cited provisions in NFPA 101 and IFC 901.7, an alarm out more than 4 hours, or sprinklers more than 10 hours, in a 24-hour period generally requires evacuation or an approved fire watch. NFPA 25 covers sprinkler impairment procedures, NFPA 72 covers fire alarm systems, and OSHA 1910.252 and NFPA 51B cover hot work during installation.

## Cutover States

**State A – Legacy active:** old system fully protecting; new system being staged. *Fire watch: not required unless hot work is underway.*

**State B – Legacy impaired, new not active:** old system disconnected in some zones; new system not yet commissioned. *Fire watch: required for affected zones once time limits apply.*

**State C – Parallel/partial:** some zones on the new system, others still impaired. *Fire watch: focus on zones not yet accepted.*

**State D – New active, pending acceptance:** new system operating but awaiting AHJ acceptance. *Fire watch: as directed by the AHJ until acceptance.*

**State E – New accepted:** project complete. *Fire watch: released.*

## Implementation Notes

- **Start coverage before State B begins**, not after the first zone goes dark
- **Use geofenced checkpoints per zone** so coverage can be adjusted as zones move from B to C to D
- **Treat covered detectors as impaired**, even if the panel shows normal
- **Pair hot work with a dedicated watch**, continuing at least 30 minutes after work ends (OSHA 1910.252)
- **Release only on AHJ approval**, with testing and notifications complete

## What Realistic Timelines Look Like

Small panel replacements may involve a few days of impairment. Large sprinkler upgrades in occupied high-rises can run for weeks, moving floor by floor. Plan coverage by zone and phase, and expect the schedule to shift.

## What to Actually Check When Comparing Providers

Ask whether the provider can scope a multi-phase project in advance. Confirm checkpoints can be updated as zones change state. Confirm hot work coverage. Ask for sample records. And confirm rates can be locked for the full project.
